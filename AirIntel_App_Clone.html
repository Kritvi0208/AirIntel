Atmospheric & Empirical Discoveries
PM2.5 Linear Coupling (r = 0.92)
PM2.5 exhibits an exceptional linear relationship with overall US AQI (r = 0.92), proving fine particulate mass is India's dominant air quality determinant.
Boundary Layer Thermal Inversion
Winter thermal inversions in the Indo-Gangetic Basin trap particulate matter beneath shallow mixing heights, causing a 3.2× AQI surge over coastal zones.
Monsoon Wet Deposition Washout
Continuous rainfall scavenging during July–August drives a nationwide ~75% drop in particulate concentrations, returning air to clean baseline tiers.
Research & Engineering Journey
Interactive 13-stage engineering flowchart detailing exact decisions, transformations, and deliverables across 4 project phases.
Phase 1 Phase 1: Ingestion & Sensor Quality Auditing (Stages 01–03) 842,160+ Records • 29 Cities • Median Imputation ▼
Stage 01: Data Ingestion & Multi-Source Extraction (01_Data_Extraction.ipynb)
Scope: 842,160+ hourly records • 29 Indian cities • 6 criteria pollutants • 7 weather variables
• Harmonized heterogeneous CAAQMS station feeds from CPCB with historical OpenWeather archives.
• Standardized timestamp schemas across differing station timezones into unified UTC/IST datetime indices.
• Key Finding: Established verified national baseline across 29 major industrial, coastal, and Gangetic basin urban corridors.
Stage 02: Data Auditing & Sensor Anomaly Detection (02_Data_Validation.ipynb)
Scope: Sensor quality • Missingness rate profiling • Sensor calibration drift
• Profiled missing value rates across all 13 chemical and meteorological channels per city and season.
• Detected negative concentration values caused by optical zero-point drift.
• Key Finding: Naively dropping missing records would have deleted >35% of valid observations, proving the necessity of an intelligent localized imputation strategy.
Stage 03: Variance-Preserving Data Cleaning (03_Data_Cleaning.ipynb)
Scope: City × Season median imputation • Limit of Detection (LOD) • Physical clipping
• Engineered City × Season Median Imputation: Avoided naive global mean imputation which distorts micro-climatic variances.
• Applied physically bounded clipping on impossible sensor spikes matching EPA and CPCB regulatory limits.
• Key Finding: Localized imputation preserved micro-climate seasonal standard deviations with zero cross-city distribution bleeding.
Phase 2 Phase 2: Atmospheric Physics & Feature Discovery (Stages 04–06) 233 Features • Harmonics • Collinearity Screening ▼
Stage 04: Atmospheric Domain Feature Engineering (04_Feature_Engineering.ipynb)
Scope: 12 base raw channels ➔ 233 engineered candidate features
• Temporal Cyclical Harmonics: Converted Hour and Month into continuous sine/cosine pairs.
• Thermodynamics & Aerosols: Formulated Temp × Humidity and Dew Point Depression.
• Atmospheric Physics: Created Planetary Boundary Layer (PBL) Stagnation flags (Wind < 5 km/h & Temp < 15°C).
• Key Finding: Atmospheric thermodynamic interactions and cyclical solar harmonics boosted tree-based split gains.
Stage 05 & 06: Advanced EDA & Statistical Screening (05_Advanced_EDA.ipynb & 06_Statistical_Analysis.ipynb)
Scope: Diurnal patterns • Hypothesis testing • Non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis
• Confirmed PM2.5 as India's dominant criterion pollutant (r = 0.92 correlation with US AQI).
• Kruskal-Wallis test confirmed regional divergence between Indo-Gangetic Plains and Peninsular India (H = 186,343.72, p < 10⁻³⁰⁰).
Phase 3 Phase 3: Machine Learning & Optuna Optimization (Stages 07–09) 11 Models • 8-Way Voting • 40.4% RMSE Drop ▼
Stages 07 & 08: Model Benchmarking & Classification (07_Regression_Models.ipynb & 08_Classification_Models.ipynb)
Scope: 11 regression models • 10 classification models • Zero leakage chronological split
• Benchmarked LightGBM, CatBoost, XGBoost, Random Forest, and regularized linear baselines.
• LightGBM achieved 0.8874 test R²; CatBoost achieved 89.4% accuracy across 6 EPA severity tiers.
Stage 09: Hyperparameter Tuning & Feature Selection (09_Optimization_Tuning.ipynb)

AirIntel: Atmospheric Data Science & Machine Learning Platform
A research initiative synthesizing 842,160+ hourly observations from India's Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) continuous monitoring network. AirIntel bridges atmospheric physics with production-grade gradient boosting to deliver continuous US AQI forecasting, 6-class EPA severity triage, and real-time TreeSHAP decision explanations under a verified Zero Data Leakage scientific protocol.
Academic Research: Ritvika CPCB CAAQMS Benchmark (842,160+ Records) LightGBM R²=0.8874 • CatBoost 89.4% MIT Open Source License
Operational Boundaries & Scientific Limitations
Rigorous scientific integrity requires explicit documentation of edge cases and operational constraints:
  1. Geographic Station Coverage (29 Urban Centers): The model is currently trained on 29 major Indian urban centers where multi-year, continuous historical CAAQMS telemetry was officially accessible and verified from CPCB archives. Broad-scale real-time open data across other Indian tier-2 and tier-3 cities could not be extracted at the time of research; extending coverage to additional nationwide corridors is actively planned for upcoming versions.
  2. Peak Spike Attenuation: Gradient boosted decision trees exhibit regression-to-the-mean during extreme episodic spikes (Actual AQI > 400). Error auditing revealed peak residuals in Gurugram (MAE 20.94) during stubble-burning episodes.
  3. Zero Fabricated Pollutants Rule: In Public Citizen Mode, the system strictly relies on ambient weather and seasonal spatial coordinates. Unmeasured chemical sensors are populated via station baseline medians rather than synthesized to ensure data governance integrity.
  4. Sensor Recalibration Drift: CAAQMS optical sensors experience baseline zero-point drifts over 5-year longitudinal periods, requiring periodic recalibration offsets.
